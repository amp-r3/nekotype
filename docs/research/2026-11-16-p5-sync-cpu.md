# P5: CPU time and D1 rows of one sync on Cloudflare

Measured on 2026-11-16 by `apps/bench` ([README](../../apps/bench/README.md)) against the Pages project `nekotype-bench` and its D1 database (region EEUR), on the Workers Free plan. Pull request 1 of [p5-plan.md](../archive/p5-plan.md); the decision it feeds is [ADR 081](../adr/081-sync-measurement-p5.md).

## Verdict

**Fail.** Every pass condition of the plan is broken, so the data model of [ADR 078](../adr/078-user-stats-targeted-updates.md) does not fit the 10 ms limit as built. The executor does not redesign: the follow-up is the maintainer's decision.

| Condition                                        | Result                                                         |
| ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Every cold request at most 8 ms                  | Failed: 13–39 ms for the three batches; 7–11 ms even for `GET` |
| Warm p95 at most 8 ms                            | Failed: 15, 21 and 15 ms for the batches                       |
| A learning sync reads at most 250 rows, writes 1 | Failed on reads: 630 rows read; writes 1                       |
| The `results` batch writes at most 151 rows      | Passed: 1 row written (see "Notes")                            |

## What was measured

- **Data.** `generateYear` of `@nekotype/shared/testing`: 229 kana with 10 recall answers each, 365 days of buckets per track with about 25 buckets a day, statistics for every kana, records for 12 modes, three devices. The row is about 350 KB (`learn` 49 KB, `learn_recent` 98 KB, `learn_buckets` about 135 KB).
- **Batches.** `learning`: 30 kana of one track, one new day. `capped`: the caps of Q1, 60 kana and 31 days per track. `results`: 50 results and their typing delta. `get`: the whole-row read of `GET /api/sync`.
- **Endpoint.** `apps/bench/src/apply-batch.ts`: the reads and writes of "Applying a batch" with the real merge and patch functions of `@nekotype/shared/runtime`. It has no session, no valibot validation, no rate limit and no version retry: **the real endpoint will cost more**, and the 8 ms line left 2 ms for them.
- **CPU time.** `cpuTime` of the tail event of `wrangler pages deployment tail --format json`. It is a whole number of milliseconds, so the percentiles are coarse. Cold: the first request after each of 5 fresh deployments. Warm: 50 further requests to the last deployment.

## Findings

1. **The snapshot response is a large share of the cost.** `get` does no merge and runs one `SELECT`, and still takes 7–11 ms cold and a median of 4 ms warm. Reading and sending a 309 KB string through the Worker is what it measures. This is the second risk of the plan ([section 8](../archive/p5-plan.md#8-risks)): a sync that carries the whole snapshot in its response starts about 4 ms (warm) to 7–11 ms (cold) in debt.
2. **The merge adds 3–5 ms warm.** `learning` and `capped` minus `get` is about 5 and 8 ms warm; `results` is about 3 ms. Cold requests add a further 5–10 ms.
3. **`json_each` counts every element as a row read (O4).** A learning batch of 30 kana and one day read 630 rows, the capped batch 1,087 (both tracks) and the `results` batch 283 (its `learn_recent` read still runs, with an empty key list). The counts are of the order of the 229 kana of `learn_recent` and the 365 day keys of a track in `learn_buckets`; the exact accounting was not isolated by a separate query. The per-key reads therefore do not avoid the row cost: at 630 rows a learning sync, the 5 million rows read a day allow about 7,900 syncs, below the 20,000 of the plan and below the 16,000 tests the write limit allows.
4. **Writes are one row.** `rows_written` is 1 for every batch, the `results` batch included: D1's meta did not report the 50 inserted results or the index rows. It is probably a limit of what `meta` reports for a `batch()`; the daily write budget therefore stays an estimate until the real endpoint is measured (PR 5).
5. **The cold-start cost is large.** Cold requests take 5–19 ms more than the warm median; the limit applies to them as to any request.

## Notes

- Tail `cpuTime` is in whole milliseconds and includes the Worker's own work only; time inside D1 is wall time.
- Every figure is from one location (a client in Tashkent, a Worker served from WAW, a database in EEUR) at one time of day.
- The first run of `measure` matched tail events by `cf-ray` including the data-centre suffix and recorded no CPU times; its D1 figures were identical to the ones above. The script was fixed and the whole run repeated; the table above is the second run.
