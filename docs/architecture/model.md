# The knowledge model

Part of the [architecture description](README.md) of the current code. [ADR 100](../adr/100-knowledge-model-fsrs-planner-and-answer-log.md) decides the model; [ADR 101](../adr/101-model-package.md) its package. The package holds FSRS-6, the kana items, the grade table, the fold with its statistics, the planner, the learn view, the forecast and the outline. `apps/web` loads it in a lazy chunk of its own (`entities/learn`, [entities.md](entities.md)); the forecast, the outline and `strongerSince` are called by the insights of `entities/learn` (P8, [ADR 112](../adr/112-insights-on-the-main-thread.md)); `reasonOf` and `introducedIn` are the data of the P8 home, and `reasonOf` is called by `reasonAt`.

## The package

`packages/model` (`@nekotype/model`) is consumed as source, like the engine. Entry points: `.` and `./testing` (event builders for tests: `intro`, `answer`, `start`, `end`; `syntheticLog` for the measurements). It runs in the browser, in a Worker and in Node (`packages/sim`). Lint enforces it: relative imports, `@nekotype/engine` and types of `@nekotype/shared/types` only; no `window`, `document`, `navigator`, storage, `fetch`, timers or `Date`; no `Math.random`. Time is passed as numbers and randomness as `Rng`. Coverage thresholds: 95% statements, functions and lines, 90% branches.

## FSRS-6

`createFsrs(weights)` gives `retrievability`, `intervalFor`, `firstMemory` and `nextMemory`; the default instance is exported. A review on the same day (`sameDay`) uses the short-term formula. `fsrs.test.ts` checks it against `FSRS6Algorithm` of ts-fsrs 6.0.0 (a dev dependency): to 6 digits on steps a day or more apart and same-day steps, to 5 digits on a history with gaps of minutes, because ts-fsrs rounds the recall probability to 8 digits and that moves a stability by about 4e-7 a step. `intervalFactor(retention)` is `intervalFor(1, retention)`.

## Items and grades

Items are derived from `kanaRows` of the engine and keyed as the app keys them (`hiragana:か`). `parts` follow [Learning](../spec/learning.md#unlocking) (が → か, きゃ → き and や, ヴァ → ヴ and ア, ヴ → ウ); `pair` links a katakana and its hiragana both ways.

`effectsOf` returns `grade` effects (`main`: the kana asked, whose phase follows; credit and pair reviews are not main) and `confusion` effects. Easy is never given. The log event types (`LogEvent` and its parts) are in `packages/shared/src/types.ts`.

## The fold

The state is never stored, synced or migrated: it is `foldLog(events, { targetMs, stats })`, or a `Fold` that takes events one by one with `apply`. The result of `foldLog` depends on the set of events only: it drops repeated `dev:n`, sorts by `compareEvents` and applies. `apply` takes an event that sorts after the last one applied and returns `applied`; a key seen before gives `duplicate`, an earlier position `out-of-order`, and in both cases nothing changes (the caller folds the whole log again on `out-of-order`).

`ModelState` holds `items` (phase, FSRS-6 memory, `lastAt`, `lastDay`, `streak`, `lastTask` per kana), `learner` (task count, gaps, sittings, task intervals, `observed`, the last 200 outcomes, the `tz` of each device), `confusions` (asked → given → count above 0) and `targetMs`. `fold.state` is live and valid until the next `apply`; `snapshot()` copies the maps and lists (and the statistics), so a later `apply` does not change it; `clone()` gives an independent fold; `clone({ stats: false })` is what the forecast will use.

Each event is applied by the rules of D5 in the [P7 plan](../archive/p7-plan.md#d5-the-fold): an event with an unknown kana is counted in `ignored` and skipped; a session start sets the device's zone; an introduction of a kana that is not new is ignored; an answer on a new kana introduces it first. A review is a `recall` on a kana that is learned before the answer; only reviews move `observed`. The same-day formula follows the local day of the writing device (`tz` of its last session start, 0 without one). A word answer has no outcome of its own (it is not on one kana) and grades the learned kana of its reading. Statistics are kept only with `stats: true` (otherwise `fold.stats` is null).

`fold.properties.test.ts` runs 200 seeded logs of 2–3 devices: any order of delivery with repeats, incremental `apply`, and devices exchanging batches with 30% failed syncs all end in the snapshot of one `foldLog` of every event.

## Statistics and the view

With `stats: true` the fold also keeps `LogStats` (`fold.stats`, live and valid until the next `apply`; `snapshot().stats`, a copy): per kana the last 10 `recall` answers (`ms`, `ok`) and the count of `recognition` answers and their errors, never decayed; per local day (the zone of the writing device) the number of answers of any task and the `recall` times of each track; the numbers of session starts and ends, and per kana `introducedIn`: the count of session starts folded when the kana entered the state (an `intro` event, or the first answer on it). An introduction is not an answer, so a day with only introductions is not active; a word answer counts in `answers` and in no kana. Days are never dropped. A day is shared between a snapshot or a clone and the live fold until the live fold changes it, and is copied then: a snapshot costs O(kana + days), not O(answers).

`describe(state, stats)` turns a state and its statistics into a `LearnView`, plain data that the screens read without loading the model: for each of the 229 kana its chart state (`locked` = no entry; `learning` = phase learning, a lapsed kana included; `learned`; `mastered` = learned with stability of at least `MASTERED_STABILITY_DAYS`, 21), `dueFrom` (the planner's, from `dueFrom` of `learner.ts`), `nextReviewAt` (`lastAt` plus the stability in days, when predicted recall reaches 0.90), the stability of the memory of every introduced kana (`nextReviewAt` stays null while learning), the session that introduced it (`introducedIn`), the recent recall answers with their median time and accuracy, the recognition counts and the confusions (kana given instead of it, highest count first, then track order). It also gives `opensNext` per track (the first new kana whose parts are learned), the mastered and introduced counts per track, and `suggestKatakana` (all 46 basic hiragana learned, ADR 100, point 5). "Due" on a screen is `dueFrom !== null && now >= dueFrom`, decided by the page with its clock; the streaks are computed from the keys of `stats.days` in `entities/learn` in a later pull request.

`strongerSince(before, after)` compares two views and lists, in track order, the kana whose chart state rose and the learned or mastered kana whose stability at least doubled.

`reasonOf(state, view, { now, track })` labels what a session does first. It calls `nextTask` once with `previous: null` and an `rng` that always draws recall (the draw only picks recall or recognition of a learning kana, which no label names), and maps the task: none → `none` (with `nextAt`, the smallest `dueFrom`), a review → `review` (with the count of kana with `dueFrom ≤ now`, both tracks), an introduction → `new` (the kana and its row, which may be of the other track under `TRACK_LAG`), any other task → `weak` (the weak learning kana in track order) or `learning` (their count). It is pure and never chooses a task: the session follows the planner whatever the label said. `isWeak` reads a `KanaView` only.

## The planner

`nextTask(state, { now, track, previous, rng })` returns the next `PlannedTask` of a session (`introduction`, `recall` with `review`, `recognition`) or null, which ends the session; the session itself (30 tasks at most, `SESSION_TASKS`) is the caller's. It reads the state and draws from `rng` only for the kind of a learning task, once. One pass over the introduced kana finds the best candidate of each list; the interval factor of the requested retention is computed once per call.

The order is: a **due** kana (learned, `lastAt + stability × intervalFactor(r) × DAY_MS ≤ now + h`, where `r` is the requested retention and `h` the median gap between sittings, 12 hours until one is known; the lowest predicted recall at `now + h` first), a **ready** learning kana (`now − lastAt ≥ STEP_MS × 2^streak`, the oldest `lastTask` first), an **introduction** while fewer than `learningLimit` kana are learning, then any **other learning** kana. Ties go to track order, hiragana first. The previous task's kana is left out of the three kana lists; when all four are empty it is taken if it is due or learning, otherwise the result is null. An introduction takes the first new kana of the selected track whose parts are all learned; the other track supplies it only when it has an introduced kana, trails the selected track by more than `TRACK_LAG` introduced kana and has an eligible one. A due kana is a recall that counts as a review; a learning kana is a recognition when the draw is below `RECOGNITION_SHARE`, otherwise a recall.

`packages/sim` runs this planner: `createProductionPolicy` in `src/policies/production.ts` maps the simulator's items to keys, keeps a `Fold` and writes each task and answer as an event; it holds no rule of the fold or the planner. `pnpm --filter @nekotype/sim run p7` prints the acceptance tables (`PART=main|planner|forecast|cost`).

## Forecast, outline and the cost

`forecast.ts` holds what P8 shows: `entities/learn` calls it in the callbacks of the `Scheduler`, with seeded generators and the settled forecasts of the device. `habitsOf(state)` gives the learner's usual sitting length, the gap between sittings, the task interval and the correct shares of learning and review answers (null while under `OUTCOMES_MIN` outcomes: the model then predicts). `nextRow(state, track)` is the first row with a kana not introduced. `forecastRow(state, row, { now, track, rng, habits, history })` plays the planner forward `FORECAST_PLAYS` times on a copy of the state and returns the sessions until every kana of the row is introduced: `raw` (the median before correction), `low`, `median` and `high`; 61 means "not within 60 sessions". A play runs sittings of the learner's usual length every `gapMs`, sessions of up to 30 tasks inside them, each task moving the clock by the usual task interval; answers are drawn from the learner's shares, or from the model's own prediction. With at least three settled forecasts in `history` (`raw` and `actual`), each result is multiplied by the median ratio of the last ten. `outlineSession` is one play of one session with the predicted answers and counts reviews, learning tasks and introductions. A play starts from `foldFromState` (`fold.ts`, not exported from the index): a fold of a copy of the state with no statistics.

`@nekotype/model/testing` exports `syntheticLog({ answers, seed })`: the planner played with a seeded learner, two five-minute sittings a day, hiragana until the 46 basic ones are learned, katakana until 80 are introduced, then hiragana. At 40,000 answers it holds both tracks and a katakana row still to come, the slowest case for the forecast.

The cost is measured in Chromium at 4× CPU throttling by `apps/web/e2e/model-cost.spec.ts` (the probe `src/app/model-probe.ts` is in the test build only): the fold of 40,000 answers with statistics within 100 ms and the forecast of the next katakana row within 200 ms, medians of five runs. `pnpm --filter @nekotype/model run cost` prints the Node timings.

Internal details keep the cost down and change no result: the fold keeps next to its `items` map an array of slots `{ key, item, track }` in the same order, which `nextTask` walks instead of the map (linked to the map by a `WeakMap`; `ModelState` is unchanged); the lists of 200 task intervals and outcomes are not shifted on every answer but assembled when read; the applied event numbers of a device are a bitmap, with a plain set for numbers that are not small non-negative integers; an answer looks its kana up once, and the zone, the event numbers of the last device and `recallsToLearn` are kept until they change; a `KanaLog` of the statistics is changed in place and copied by `snapshot()` and `clone()`, and the day of the last answer is kept. The fold and its lists are classes, not closures: every fold shares one `apply`, so the optimized `foldLog` and the forecast's plays do not fall back to slower code on each new fold.
