# ADR 086: The CPU Line of the Real Sync Endpoint Is a Warm Median of 8 ms (12 ms at the Caps)

- **Context:** [ADR 082](082-sync-acknowledgement-without-snapshot.md) set the pass line of the real endpoint at a warm median of 5 ms for a learning sync and for 50 results and 8 ms for the batch at the caps, from the model `keyed` (3, 4 and 6 ms). P5 PR 5 measured the real endpoint on `nekotype-bench` ([research note](../research/2026-11-18-p5-sync-endpoint.md)): 8, 8 and 11 ms, over the line. No request was answered with error 1102 or with an outcome other than `ok`; a learning sync reads 5 rows, 50 results read 52. The causes were looked for before the line was moved:
  - a `GET` with a session and nothing else takes 2 ms warm (routing, the session lookup, the response), which the model did not include;
  - the compute of a learning batch measured in Node takes under 1 ms: validation 0.15–0.7 ms, parsing and guarding the small columns 0.2 ms, the merges and the serialization 0.2 ms. Replacing valibot with hand-written guards would save a fraction of a millisecond;
  - the rest is the cost of the calls to D1 and of the platform, which the code cannot lower except by dropping statements the endpoint needs.
- **Decision:** The maintainer approved this course on 2026-11-18 ("do it by the plan": measure, then decide by the numbers).
  1. For the real endpoint the warm median of `cpuTime` is at most 8 ms for a learning sync and for 50 results and at most 12 ms for the batch at the caps; a learning sync reads at most 250 rows; no request of the measurement is answered with error 1102 or has an outcome other than `ok`. PR 13 repeats the measurement against this line. Cold requests (19–49 ms) and the warm p95 (17–32 ms) are recorded and do not gate, as in ADR 082.
  2. valibot stays: it is not what costs the time, and the schemas are the single source of the bounds.
- **Justification:** The line of ADR 082 was derived from a model without the session and the validation; the real endpoint adds what any authenticated endpoint has. A request that takes 8 ms of the 10 ms warm is still under the limit, and the tolerance the ADR relies on for cold requests has been observed again (every measured request, up to 49 ms, none cut off).
- **Consequences:** Less headroom than ADR 082 planned: a typical sync uses most of the limit, so any later work in the handler must be measured. If error 1102 appears in production, the choice stays the maintainer's: Workers Paid, or less work in the handler (the candidates are fewer statements per sync and a cheaper session check).
- **Status:** In force. Amends the pass line of ADR 082 (point 7); its other points stand.
- **Status:** Point 2 amended by [ADR 102](102-sync-format-3-server.md) for the events of sync format 3 only: their bounds are one hand-written guard, not a valibot schema. valibot still checks the envelope.
