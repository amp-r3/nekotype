# ADR 093: Nekotype Is a Learning App: the Typing Trainer Goes, Words Join Learning, and an Adaptive Knowledge Model, Kana Sounds and Mnemonics Come Before the Release

- **Context:** After P5 the product has two parts: learning kana on `/` ([ADR 058](058-learning-first.md)) and a romaji typing trainer on `/type` with tests by time and count, kana per minute, personal bests, a results screen, typing statistics on `/stats` and their sync. On 19 November 2026 the maintainer decided that Nekotype teaches Japanese, starting with kana, and that the typing trainer does not serve that goal. The maintainer also found the learning itself too rigid: a kana needs 10 recall answers before it can be confident, so a new kana takes about 1.3 sessions of 25 tasks whatever the learner knows, hiragana takes about 130 sessions, and a learner who already reads kana has no way to skip ahead. The users are mostly beginners, but also people who already know some kana. The maintainer expects a learner to spend 10–15 minutes a day, wants the sound of each kana from the first lesson, although no source of recordings is chosen yet (O6), and wants vocabulary with meanings in the first release. Russian translations of words have no source with enough coverage (O3), and the maintainer does not expect one. Nobody but the maintainer uses production until the release tag ([ADR 061](061-schema-reset-before-release.md)), so the local schema and the D1 tables can still change without migrations of user data.
- **Decision:**
  1. **Positioning.** Nekotype is a keyboard-first app for learning Japanese, starting with hiragana and katakana from zero. There is no typing trainer: no route, no tests by time, count or zen, no kana per minute, no personal bests and no typing statistics.
  2. **What goes** (phase P6): the `/type` route and its page; typing sessions, test end, results and records; the results screen and the repeat; `KanaStat` and word weights; the typing section of `/stats` with its filters, test chart, history and weak kana table; the typing settings; the typing part of sync (`results`, `records`, typing keys of `user_stats`, server history) and the D1 table `results` with the typing columns of `user_stats`, removed by a D1 migration. The separation of learning and typing data (A39) has nothing left to separate.
  3. **What stays:** the input engine with stream matching, the romaji buffer and both strictness modes, because words are typed in learning too; the input source and its layout and IME notices; the content pipeline with its committed word and phrase chunks; `/api/reports` and the `content_reports` table. The report dialog comes back with word tasks. The web build ships a content chunk only when a feature uses it, and `/about` credits what is shipped: phrases (Tatoeba) are kept in the pipeline for later lessons and are not shipped while nothing uses them.
  4. **Words join learning** in two steps: words made only of kana the learner knows, as practice of the kana in context, each answer counting for every kana of the word (the engine already attributes errors and timings per kana); then words as vocabulary, learned with their meanings. Meanings are English only, from JMdict ([ADR 069](069-word-readings-and-meanings.md)); the interface stays English and Russian.
  5. **An adaptive knowledge model** replaces the fixed confidence rule and the Leitner boxes before the release: the pace follows what the learner shows, and a placement test lets a learner who already knows kana start where they are. The model (memory state per kana and per word, the weight of each kind of answer, unlocking, the session planner and how two devices merge its state) is decided by a separate ADR after a research note that compares the candidates on simulated learners against the [learning goals](../spec/README.md#learning-goals): about 10–15 minutes a day, the 46 basic hiragana in about 7 days, at least 90% correct reviews, placement in at most 3 minutes. It is one model for kana and words, with recall, recognition and listening tasks, so the later phases add items and task types without changing the stored state of a kana. Until then [learning.md](../spec/learning.md) stays in force. The rejection of FSRS for kana in ADR 058 is reopened by this research.
  6. **Sound from the first kana.** The sound of each kana is part of learning from its introduction, and listening (the sound is played, the learner picks the kana) is a task type. Audio comes right after the model, as soon as the source of the recordings (O6) is chosen; the search for it runs alongside P6 and P7.
  7. **Mnemonics.** A beginner meets each kana with a short mnemonic for its shape, written for the project in English and Russian and checked by the maintainer (O9).
  8. **Phases.** P0–P5 are done and do not change. The remaining phases: P6 learning only (removing typing), P7 the knowledge model, P8 audio (the former P6) with listening tasks, P9 the start (placement test and mnemonics), P10 words in learning, P11 vocabulary with meanings, P12 polish and release (the former P7). If O6 is still open when P7 closes, P9 goes before P8. Only P6 is specified in full; P7–P12 are specified when the previous phase closes.
- **Justification:**
  - A speed trainer serves people who already read kana, a different audience with a different goal; its numbers compete with learning progress for the learner's attention, and its data doubles the stores, the sync and the statistics that every later change has to carry.
  - Words test a kana where it is used, next to other kana, and they are the first step from kana to the language. The engine's per-kana attribution makes a word answer evidence about each of its kana, so word tasks feed the knowledge model instead of a separate statistic.
  - Removing typing first leaves the knowledge model one kind of data to model and one sync path to change.
  - Sound and mnemonics serve the beginner from the first kana: the sound tells how a kana is pronounced, which romaji only approximates, and a mnemonic gives the shape something to hold on to; both are cheap next to the model and change no stored state.
  - Words a learner can read with the kana they know are the first visible return of learning kana, so they are shown as progress, not only used as tasks.
  - The model comes before the release because after it every schema change needs a migration (ADR 061), and a new model of the learning state is the largest schema change ahead.
- **Rejected alternatives:**
  - _Keeping `/type` as free practice without statistics:_ code, routes and budgets kept for a mode with no place in the learning path.
  - _Removing the phrases with the trainer:_ they are verified, credited content for later lessons, and the pipeline costs nothing at runtime.
  - _Russian meanings:_ no source covers the word list well enough (O3); a partial translation would mix languages within one session.
  - _Audio after vocabulary, as the last phase before the release:_ a beginner would learn every kana without hearing it.
  - _Vocabulary after the release (v1.1):_ an earlier release, but the maintainer wants the first release to teach words with meanings; the item model of P7 keeps either order possible.
  - _Tuning the current rule (fewer than 10 answers, unlocking in groups) without a model:_ it shortens the path for everyone at once and still cannot place a learner who already knows kana.
