# ADR 058: Learning First, with Adaptive Unlocking of Kana

- **Status:** In force. Amended by [ADR 061](061-schema-reset-before-release.md): a saved `content: 'single'` is not converted and `/learn` has no 301. Supersedes [ADR 019](019-runtime-fallback-for-vocabulary-pools-in-buildpool.md) and [ADR 028](028-empty-pool-falls-back-to-basic-rows.md) from P3. Amends [ADR 031](031-adopt-the-nekotype-design-system.md), [ADR 037](037-router-and-application-shell.md), [ADR 046](046-trainer-lifecycle-and-content-loading.md), [ADR 048](048-worst-keystroke-latency-gate.md) and [ADR 055](055-test-screen-focus-and-chrome.md). Amended by [ADR 076](076-stats-screen-redesign.md): `/stats` gets an activity map and a weak kana table again, and its charts are no longer one line each.
- **Context:** The MVP was specified as a romaji speed trainer modelled on Monkeytype, for people who already read kana. Learning was a secondary mode planned for P5 and the first candidate for deferral (A10), and the default exercise was a speed test on single kana, scored in kana per minute. Such a test asks the learner to show that they know a kana by typing its romaji, which rewards spelling it letter by letter: the learner remembers "n-a" rather than "na", and the score is mostly typing speed. Romaji is still how Japanese is typed and a real skill for words and phrases, but it does not measure knowing a kana. P0–P2 are finished and P3 has not started, so no learning, metrics or storage code depends on the old plan.
- **Decision:**
  1. **Positioning.** The core of the product is learning hiragana and katakana from zero, with progress tracking; typing words and phrases in romaji is the second part. Speed tests on single kana are removed with their metrics: content `single`, the row selection and the fallback kana pool go away in P3, and a saved `content: 'single'` becomes `words`.
  2. **Screens.** `/` is the learning screen: the kana chart of the selected track, "Continue" and "Review (N)". Typing moves to `/type`. `/learn`, the address of the learning stub, redirects to `/` with a 301.
  3. **Tasks.** Kana are learned by recall (the kana is shown and its romaji typed, relaxed by default) and recognition (the romaji is shown and the kana chosen from four by click or keys 1–4). The engine does not change.
  4. **Adaptive unlocking,** as in keybr. Hiragana and katakana are independent tracks. Each starts with the five vowels, and the next kana in table order unlocks when every unlocked kana of the track is confident: the median answer time of its last 10 recall answers is within a target (1500 ms by default) and at least 9 of them are correct. A kana built from others waits until they are confident. Only recall answers count, and the recall answer time leaves out the typing of the spelling (A37). Tasks favour weak kana, and a new kana is in focus until it has 10 recall answers.
  5. **Retention.** Leitner boxes stay: a kana enters box 1 when it first becomes confident and is then reviewed on a daily schedule. Unlocking depends on confidence, not on boxes.
  6. **Typing** is measured in kana per minute and kana accuracy, compared with the user's average and personal best. Typing statistics never change the learning state, and learning never changes typing statistics.
  7. **Audio** is designed now and built in a phase of its own: an `AudioProvider` plays the recorded sound of a kana after each task.
  8. **Phases.** P3 learning, P4 words and phrases, P5 backend and sync, P6 audio, P7 polish and release. P0–P2, their acceptance and the deferral of the device checks to P7 do not change.
- **Justification:** Learning from zero is where knowledge of the kana is built, and a learner who knows them can then practise words. Recall by typing keeps romaji as the way to answer, while the answer time measures recall rather than typing. One new kana at a time keeps the working set small for a beginner, and confidence, which needs both speed and accuracy, is a better gate than the two correct answers that used to move a kana to box 1. Alternatives rejected:
  - keeping single-kana speed tests as a second mode: the exercise would keep rewarding letter-by-letter spelling, and its score would compete with the learning progress;
  - unlocking a whole row when its kana reach box 1 (A12): five new kana at once overload a beginner, and two correct answers say nothing about speed;
  - flashcards without typing: the learner needs romaji to type Japanese at all, and recall is a stronger test than recognition;
  - timing recall to the first key or to the last key (A37);
  - an SRS such as FSRS for kana: boxes are already specified and easy to explain, and the set of kana is small.
- **Consequences:**
  - The specification is rewritten for this direction: `learning.md`, `metrics.md` and `phases.md` entirely; `README.md`, `frontend.md`, `storage-sync.md`, `backend.md`, `architecture.md`, `quality.md`, `backlog.md` and `decisions.md` (A35–A44; A10, A12 and A24 superseded) in part. The design system needs a learning screen, a state mode of `KanaChart` and new screens; the list is in the [design README](../design/README.md).
  - Code of P0–P2 that goes or changes: in P3, content `single`, the row selection (ADR 019, ADR 028) and the fallback kana pool (ADR 046), while the trainer moves to `/type` and the input source to `shared`; in P4, WPM, raw WPM, the per-second chart and the per-kana reaction time of the results screen.
  - The initial bundle carries the learning screen instead of the typing screen, which becomes a lazy route; staying within the 35 KB budget is part of the P3 acceptance.
