# ADR 082: A Sync Is Acknowledged Without the Snapshot, `learn` Is Updated per Key, and the CPU Line Is the Typical Request

- **Context:** [ADR 081](081-sync-measurement-p5.md) recorded that the model of [ADR 078](078-user-stats-targeted-updates.md) fails the limits of `p5-plan.md` and left the follow-up open. A second measurement compared three variants on Cloudflare ([research note](../research/2026-11-16-p5-sync-variants.md)):
  - the snapshot in the response costs about 4 ms of a warm request, and `json_each` reads 630 rows for a learning sync;
  - the variant `keyed` (no snapshot, `json_extract` with bound paths, `learn` patched per key) takes 3 ms in the warm median for a learning sync, 6 ms for the batch at the caps of Q1 and 4 ms for 50 results, and reads 6 rows;
  - no variant passes "every cold request and the warm p95 at most 8 ms": `keyed` takes 9–23 ms cold and 10–15 ms at the warm p95, and a handler that does nothing has a warm p95 of 3 ms and a maximum of 5 ms;
  - none of the 990 requests of the two measurements, the slowest 50 ms, was answered with error 1102.
- **Decision:** The maintainer chose the first option of the research note on 2026-11-16.
  1. **Acknowledgement.** `POST /api/sync` answers `{ lastSeq, version }`, where `version` is `user_stats.version` after the request. The response carries no snapshot, and the `UPDATE` returns no column.
  2. **The client applies its own batch.** The client stores the `version` of its snapshot. When the acknowledgement has `version` = stored + 1, nothing but this batch was written: the client applies the batch to its snapshot with the function of `shared/runtime` that the server uses, called with the server's arguments, and stores the result with the new `version`. With any other `version` (another device wrote, or the device has no snapshot yet, which includes `seq` = 1) it reads the snapshot from the server.
  3. **`GET /api/sync` never carries the whole row.** Without `part` it answers `{ lastSeq, version }`, read by the key; the client calls it on `start` and `online` and reads the snapshot only when `version` differs from the stored one. `?part=rest`, `?part=recent` and `?part=buckets` each answer one part with `version`: everything but the two large columns (with the settings and `lastSeq`), `learn_recent`, and `learn_buckets`. The client stores the three parts only when their `version` is the same, and reads them again otherwise.
  4. **Reads by path.** The touched keys are read with `json_extract(column, ?, ?, …)`, one bound path per key and at most 98 paths per statement, not with `json_each`. This replaces point 2 of ADR 078 for the reads; the writes stay `json_patch`.
  5. **`learn` per key.** `learn` is no longer parsed whole: `unlocked` and `activeDays` are read by path, the touched `entries` and `stats` too, and the column is written with `json_patch`. This replaces point 3 of ADR 078 for `learn`; `kana_stats`, `counters`, `typing_records` and `device_seq` stay whole. A merge patch merges objects key by key, so the patch of a kana's `stats` names with `null` every `mixups` key that the stored value has and the merged value has not.
  6. **The newest bucket day is stored.** A column `bucket_newest` holds the newest day of `learn_buckets` per track. The 365-day rule needs it, and reading the day keys with `json_each` costs 365 rows per track. The patch of a track names with `null` the days between the old and the new lower bound of the window, at most 366.
  7. **The pass line is the typical request.** For the real endpoint on the year-long row: the warm median of `cpuTime` is at most 5 ms for a learning sync and for 50 results and at most 8 ms for the batch at the caps; a learning sync reads at most 250 rows; and no request of the measurement is answered with error 1102 or has an outcome other than `ok`. Cold requests and the warm p95 are measured and recorded, and do not gate. This replaces the line of ADR 081 and amends O4: the design keeps the typical request under the limit and leaves cold requests and the tail to the platform's tolerance.
  8. **The caps of Q1 are confirmed** at 60 kana and 31 bucket days per track: the batch at the caps passes point 7 in the `keyed` variant.
  9. **A request that is cut off is an ordinary failure.** A sync is idempotent by `seq`, so the batch stays in `outbox` and is sent again by the rules of "Failed requests".
- **Justification:**
  - Points 1–6 remove what the measurement found: the response (4 ms), the whole-`learn` parse (2 ms) and the row cost (630 → 6). The typical sync then uses a third of the limit, with room for the session and the validation.
  - The line of ADR 081 cannot be met by a model: the cold cost is that of a new isolate running the handler for the first time (an empty handler takes 1–5 ms cold), and the warm tail is there for a handler that does nothing. Holding the phase for it would not make a sync cheaper.
  - The tolerance is observed, not assumed: 990 requests up to five times over the limit, none cut off. Point 9 makes a cut-off request cost a retry and no data.
  - Point 2 adds no rule: the view of the client already is the snapshot merged with the outbox by the server's rules, so the client computes what the server stored. A property test keeps the two equal.
- **Consequences:**
  - **Risk accepted.** With few users many syncs meet a cold isolate, so a large share of production requests will run over 10 ms and rely on the tolerance. If error 1102 appears in production, the choice is the maintainer's: Workers Paid, or less work in the handler. How it is noticed (the Functions metrics of the Pages project, the client's failed-sync state) is confirmed in PR 13.
  - A device that sees another device's write makes four requests (the acknowledgement or the check, and three parts) instead of one. The daily request budget carries it.

- **Status:** In force. Amends ADR 078 (points 2 and 3) and replaces the pass line of ADR 081. Point 7 is amended by [ADR 086](086-sync-endpoint-cpu-line.md): the real endpoint's line is 8 ms (12 ms at the caps).
