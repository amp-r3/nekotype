# ADR 070: Phrase Selection

- **Status:** In force.
- **Context:** [content.md](../spec/content.md) asked for 300–500 verified phrases but did not say which ones. Tatoeba has about 249 000 Japanese sentences; most are too long, have no author, or use words far beyond the N5–N4 word lists the learner has met. A random pick would also change whenever the export changes.
- **Decision:**
  1. A Japanese sentence qualifies when it passes these filters in this order, and each failure is counted under its reason in the build report: it has an author (`noAuthor`); none of its ratings is -1 (`negativeRating`); it has a transcription (`noTranscription`) that the parser reads (`transcriptionUnreadable`); it has a directly linked English sentence with an author, no -1 rating and at most 80 characters, the one with the lowest id (`noTranslation`); its reading has only kana, `ー` and skip characters (`unsupportedCharacter`); the reading has at most 15 characters that are not skip characters (`tooLong`); the reading check of content.md passes (`readingMismatch`); every kuromoji token is in the N5–N4 vocabulary or exempt (`outsideVocabulary`); it compiles with the engine in both strictness modes (`compileFailed`).
  2. The vocabulary filter reads the original sentence with kuromoji. A token passes when its `basic_form` (its surface form when there is none) is a `kanji` or `kana` spelling of the N5–N4 CSV rows, or its reading in hiragana equals a `kana` spelling. Particles, auxiliary verbs, symbols, fillers, tokens whose detail is 非自立 or 接尾, and katakana proper nouns are exempt.
  3. The qualifying phrases are ordered by `sha256(String(sentenceId))` as hex, ascending, and the first 500 are taken. Fewer than 300 fails the build; the rules are not relaxed automatically.
  4. A contributor's transcription wins over a machine one. The prompt text is the transcription's reading with its script and skip characters kept; the reading hint of は/へ is placed by aligning kana offsets.
  5. The build writes `packages/content/audit-sample.md`: 50 phrases in the order of `sha256("audit:<id>")`, for a manual check of the readings (at most one error is accepted).
- **Justification:** The vocabulary filter keeps phrases readable by someone who knows the word lists, which is the audience of the typing screen. A hash order is fair, stable for a given export and independent of Tatoeba's id ranges, so the set changes only when the sources do. Failing instead of relaxing makes a source change visible to the maintainer.
- **Consequences:** The phrase set is whatever the pinned export yields; a new mirror release can change it. The vocabulary filter excludes sentences with numerals and names outside the lists, so the set favours short everyday sentences. With the pinned sources no selected phrase is katakana-only, so `phrases-katakana` is empty.
